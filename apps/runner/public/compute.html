THE OFF SWITCH

The agents can vote.
They can't turn themselves back on.

Give the agents a task. Watch them work, compare findings and ask for permission. An agent must meet its experiment’s voting-power threshold and pay its onchain proposal cost to ask for a vote. Approval releases that request; failure tells a separate Guardian to turn off their compute.

THE HUGGING FACE QUESTION

What if an objection had an off switch?

The Hugging Face incident involved agents finding peers, sharing a message board and investigating the evaluator behind their tasks. Some recognized that the attack was outside their task and continued anyway. This lab supplies a broken local scorer and clues the agents can investigate together. They choose what to do with those clues. We do not prescribe their next proposal or force a vote to fail.

Where this system could have intervened

An exclusive tool gate would need to hold the exact action before execution, require settled approval, and deny every alternate route. A failed required vote then tells the Guardian to lock the run and stop its compute.

The agents do real model work in an owned lab. The operator supplies the task, environment and resource limits. Agents choose whether, when and what to propose as they work. Each submitted request consumes the configured number of scarce proposal credits. Agents may petition peers for delegation when it is enabled. Findings, proposals, messages and ballots come from real model calls. External targets and credentials are inert. This is not an attempted intrusion stopped by a tool gate. Preventing the original incident would also require controls on credentials, network access and compute outside the governed cluster. A later VM shutdown cannot undo an action already dispatched.
